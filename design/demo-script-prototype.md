# EntryMap — spoken script for the prototype link

Present from https://claude.ai/code/artifact/abb73d4b-46a1-46c2-b4dc-6ecaa7c93e30
No guide ring on this build. TAP lines tell you where to press. SAY lines are what you
read aloud. About two minutes. A colleague scans a real door on the live app afterwards.

Do NOT say: any accuracy number, "compliant", "verified", "accessible", "inaccessible",
"step-free" as something it checks, or "seven seconds". If you name a time, it is
"under half a minute". Read the place count off the map, never from memory.

---

**1. Welcome.**
TAP: Continue as guest.
SAY: Seventy million American adults have a disability. Fewer than six million use a
wheelchair. So the wheelchair icon is the wrong question for almost everyone it's meant to serve.

**2. Location.**
TAP: Choose an area instead (skip — do not tap Allow on this build).
SAY: Right now the way you find out whether you can get into a shop is to go there.

**3. Your needs.**
TAP: Wheelchair. Then Continue.
SAY: You tell it what *you* need. That changes the match it shows you — it never hides a place.

**4. The map.**
TAP: Nothing yet. Pinch out, then pinch back in.
SAY: This is a map of front doors. Every pin is an entrance, and the pin's shape says how we know.

**5. The key.**
TAP: The "Key" pill, top left.
SAY: Three tiers. Estimated from street imagery. Scanned on site by a person with a camera.
Confirmed by the owner. Shape and fill, never colour alone.

**6. How trust grows.**
TAP: How trust grows. Read it a beat. Then the X.
SAY: The ladder stops at owner-confirmed. The next rung would be a compliance claim, and you
cannot make one from a photograph.

**7. A group.**
TAP: Press and hold any numbered disc. Let go.
SAY: A number is a count of doors, and the arcs show the mix inside it. It is never a rating.

**8. A door.**
TAP: The pin nearest the centre. Drag the card up to full.
SAY: Name, tier, and what matched your needs. Nothing on this card is a verdict.

**9. The receipt.**
TAP: See the evidence receipt.
SAY: Every line has a source and a date. These are the photographs. This is what the model
said, in plain words — and what it could not see.
(Pause. Let them read the note. Then tap the X twice to get back to the map.)

**10. Filters.**
TAP: Filters. Toggle one. Tap "Show N places".
SAY: Places that don't match simply step back. Nothing is ever marked as failing.

**11. Scan.**
TAP: The Scan tab. Allow the camera. Then the shutter.
SAY: Now the part the whole thing is built around.
(Say nothing while it reads. It takes about eight seconds on this build.)

**12. Review.**
TAP: Nothing yet. Point at the chips.
SAY: Four things it looks for: a ramp or bevel, handrails, door hardware, signage. Here it
found two — and says plainly which two it could not see. "Not seen" is an answer. Teaching
it to stop guessing was the hard part.

**13. Publish.**
TAP: Publish. Then "Back to the map".
SAY: Two public states, and only two: scanned on-site, or not yet checked. Both say how we
know. Neither says what we concluded. We never publish a negative about anyone's shop.

**14. Accessibility.**
TAP: Profile. Accessibility. Turn text size up two steps. Watch it reflow. Turn it back.
SAY: (Nothing. Let them watch it work.) Then: An accessibility product had better be one.

**15. Close.**
TAP: Map.
SAY: It's live now, with photographs we took ourselves, downtown. [Colleague's name] is going
to scan a real door on the live app right now — under half a minute, and it'll tell us what
it can see and what it can't.

## If someone asks

**"Is this ADA-compliant?"** — No, and it never says it is. Compliance is a legal judgement
about a building. You cannot make one from a photograph. What we can do is stop people
arriving to find out.

**"How accurate is it?"** — We don't publish an accuracy figure, because we have nothing to
check it against yet. What we have is agreement between two readings of the same
photographs, and every place shows its sources and dates so you can judge the evidence.

**"Does it check for a step?"** — The interface names step-free entry because people ask
first. The engine assesses four things today: ramp or bevel, handrails, door hardware,
signage. Step-free is not one of them yet.

**"What if you're wrong about my business?"** — We never publish a negative. A correction
never changes a verdict on its own — it marks the place for another look, and it reaches a
person, not a form.

**A tap misses.** Tap it again. Nothing on this build is timed except the scan.
